# 017 Judge the step, not the note

**Decision.** In Your turn, Merel judges each step, not each note. The first note is wherever you start. Each note after counts when you move from the note found last the right way (up or down) by roughly the right amount: from half the step to 200 cents past it, or half its size past it for a leap. A whole step counts from 100 to 400 cents, a half step from 50 to 300, do to sol from 350 to 1050. An octave either way is the same note. A wrong note does not count and resets nothing: Merel says so mildly and you try that step again. Only a long silence (3 s) ends a try, and you start again from do; so does Hear it. Luk, 2026-11-16, after 0.2.0 was tried by someone else: still too frustrating, and "if the pitch goes in the required direction with more or less the right interval, it's ok". This replaces the tolerance of a semitone from the note in [[016-movable-do]], and its rule that a wrong note starts the try again from do ([[014-your-turn-earns-the-dot]]).

**Why.** People who do not sing squeeze and stretch steps, mix up half steps and whole steps, and drift. A semitone from where the note should be is a test a trained singer passes and a beginner fails again and again, and starting over from do at every wrong note made each slip cost the whole scale. What a beginner learns first is the shape: up, down, a small step or a leap. Judged on that, they hear themselves get it right, and the voice dot still shows them where they really are.

**What it costs.** A scale sung in half steps all the way, or in steps a little too large, passes. A step barely sung (less than half its size) is not one, so a singer who sings the same note twice runs a note behind and sings one more. Leaps are told apart from steps only roughly: do to re to mi passes for do mi sol if the steps are large. Rejected: keeping the note judge with a wider tolerance (drift and squeezed steps still add up); judging only the direction (do re mi would pass for do mi sol, and exercise 3 would mean little).
