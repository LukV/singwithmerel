# 016 Your first note is do

**Decision.** In Your turn, do is wherever you start. The first note of a try, held 0.2 s, is the melody's first note, and every note after is judged from it, within a semitone, in any octave. Do follows you as you go: the key is the average of where you sang the notes found so far. Off key, you start again from do, and may start somewhere else. Merel still sings in C. Luk, 2026-11-15, after his own first try: from his voice, C3 is low and C4 too high to reach sol.

**Why.** A singer's comfortable range is not Merel's. Judging in Merel's C made a man choose between a do too low and a sol too high, and the recordings show untrained singers start in their own key anyway: the child near A♭ and G, the woman near F♯, the man near E♭. Relative pitch is what the scale teaches; in do major the names stay the same ([[008-do-major]]).

**What it costs.** A first note cannot be wrong, so a wrong start is not caught; the notes after it are. Measured from the first note, a take whose first note is a little off has its other notes further off: the man's scale up, whose do was 72 cents flat of the rest, now stops at a fa a whole tone above mi, which Merel's C let through. The voice dot on the staff follows your own do; before the first note it sits on that note.
